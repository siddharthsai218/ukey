Built for paranoid security.

Software authenticator apps compromise your keys by design. uKey binds everything into dedicated hardware silicon.

HARDWARE SILICON VS SOFTWARE PASSKEYS

Why Bluetooth Passkey Apps Fail.

Apps like sn.mahir.passkey emulate generic Bluetooth keyboards over the air and store cryptographic keys in phone SQLite databases. Any malware or root exploit can exfiltrate them in milliseconds. In contrast, uKey keys are generated inside Google Titan M2 or Samsung Knox StrongBox (EAL5+ certified). Private keys never touch RAM, never touch flash, and cannot be exported even by root.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Everything you need to know.

What happens if my phone battery dies? Will I be locked out of sudo?
No, you will never be locked out. uKey’s PAM module is configured as sufficient. If your phone is unplugged, powered off, or disconnected, uKey immediately yields to the Linux password prompt in 0.0 seconds. You simply press Enter or type your password as usual.
How is this different from Bluetooth passkey apps on the Play Store?
Apps like sn.mahir.passkey emulate generic Bluetooth keyboards over the air. They do not emulate kernel FIDO2 security keys, cannot do Linux PAM sudo elevation, cannot sign OpenSSH keys, and store credentials in software userland. uKey binds keys into Android StrongBox hardware silicon and creates an authentic kernel /dev/uhid device on Linux.
Is local Wi-Fi pairing safe on public coffee shop networks?
Yes. When using Wi-Fi, every packet must include an authentic HMAC token hash generated using a paired 6-digit cryptographic PIN. If an unauthorized attacker or rogue scanner on the LAN attempts to hit port 8443, the daemon drops the connection in under 4ms with zero processing.
